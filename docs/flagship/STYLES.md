# The house styles

Six styles, every one of them lifted off a real build Sarah approved. Pick ONE
per client and commit to it. Rotating is the point: a fleet where every site
shares a look is a template, and an owner can tell.

These are not invented. Each entry lists the build it came from, and its palette,
type pairing and section skeleton are what that build actually shipped.

## What every one of them carries

Profiled across the approved builds, these appear in nearly all of them, and in
none of the ones Sarah rejected. They are not decoration, they are the difference
between a site and a template:

| element | in the approved builds |
| --- | --- |
| a marquee | 5 of 5 |
| a live counter | 5 of 5 |
| an accordion FAQ | 4 of 5 |
| a drag or slider moment | 4 of 5 |
| a proof section using their REAL rating | 5 of 5 |
| parallax or a sticky stage | 3 of 5 |
| three type families | 4 of 5 |

The rejected builds averaged one font family, one photograph, and no proof
section at all. One shipped with no webfont whatsoever.

## Their real reputation is the proof section

The brief now carries `THEIR REAL REPUTATION`: their true star rating and review
count, held on roughly 4,400 leads and until 2026-08-22 thrown away before the
builder ever saw it. Use the real number, prominently, and never round it up.

Invented review TEXT stays banned. If the mined evidence carries a genuine
quoted review, use it with attribution. If it does not, show the rating and the
count on their own, which is honest and still the most persuasive thing on the
page. A proof section that is missing is worse than one that is only numbers.

## 1. STEEL AND EMBER
*From: Wild Horse Construction & Concrete*

Warm off-white ground, one hot orange accent, a fat slab display face with a
handwritten annotation face for margins and callouts.

- ground `#F5F3EE`, accent `#DD4A17`
- **Ultra** display, **Archivo** body, **Caveat** for handwritten asides
- work > services > estimate > proof > faq
- carries: photo slider, counter, marquee, accordion, a tool cursor

For trades that pour, weld, frame and finish. The handwriting face is what stops
it reading corporate, and it is the detail most worth keeping.

## 2. NIGHT NEON
*From: Huck Yeah*

Near-black ground, one electric orange, condensed all-caps display, canvas
ambience behind the hero.

- ground `#101614`, accent `#ff5a1f`
- **Anton** display, **Archivo** body
- why > fleet > play > rates > desk > area > contact
- carries: slider, counter, marquee, canvas, parallax, a tool cursor

For rentals, tours, powersports, anything sold on fun. The only style where a
canvas effect earns its place.

## 3. BARBER RED
*From: Columbia Falls Barbershop*

Cream ground, deep barber red, condensed display over a workhorse serif. The
most classically "main street" of the six.

- ground `#F4EDE1`, accent `#B0272C`
- **Anton** display, **Oswald** subheads, **Source Serif 4** body
- top > shop > menu > word > faq > book
- carries: slider, counter, marquee, accordion, sticky nav

For barbers, salons, tattoo, butchers, cobblers, any trade with a shopfront and
a price list. The menu section is the hero of this layout, not the photograph.

## 4. HIGHWAY AMBER
*From: Hungry Horse Motel*

Deep blue-black ground, amber signage accent, an editorial serif against a
condensed display. Reads like a lit sign on a dark road.

- ground `#0B1018`, accent `#FF9A3D`
- **Anton** display, **Instrument Serif** editorial, **Inter** body
- top > stay > road > rooms > word > faq > book
- carries: counter, marquee, accordion, parallax
- the only approved build at 8 distinct photographs, and it shows

For motels, lodges, campgrounds, diners, anywhere along a road. Photography
carries this one, so it needs the most distinct frames of any style.

## 5. FIELD NOTE
*From: Sands Surveying*

Bone ground, rust accent, a mono face used as a real instrument rather than a
gimmick: coordinates, measurements, plat numbers.

- ground `#F4F1E8`, accent `#A84718`
- **Archivo** display, **IBM Plex Mono** data, **Source Serif 4** body
- line > surveys > finder > reviews > faq > book
- carries: slider, counter, marquee, accordion, parallax, sticky

For surveyors, engineers, inspectors, arborists, anyone whose work produces
figures. The mono face is the whole personality; use it for anything numeric and
nowhere else.

## 6. WILD REVERENT
*From: the Wild Hope Church concept*

Deep ink, ember accent, a variable serif at enormous display size.

- ground `#0B1016`, accent `#E08A3C`
- **Fraunces** display, **Inter** body
- hero > the turn > story > the week > life > honest > visit
- carries: THE TURN, a drag reel, a horizontal rail, an oversized wordmark

For churches, retreats, galleries, guides, anything sold on feeling rather than
price. The scroll-driven turn is the centrepiece; see `MOTION.md`.

## Choosing

Read the trade, then the town, then the owner's own words. Pick the style that
is TRUE about them, not the one you like:

- pours, builds, welds, finishes -> Steel and Ember
- sold on fun, rented by the day -> Night Neon
- shopfront with a price list -> Barber Red
- along a road, photographed at night -> Highway Amber
- produces figures and documents -> Field Note
- sold on feeling -> Wild Reverent

Two rules on top. Do not put the same style on two businesses in the same trade
in the same town, because they will see each other's. And when two styles fit,
take the one with the type pairing you can execute best, since the fonts do more
work here than the layout does.

Never invent a seventh by blending two. A hybrid reads as neither.
