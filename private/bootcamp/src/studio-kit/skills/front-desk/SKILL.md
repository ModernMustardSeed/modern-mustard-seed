# The Front Desk

The first reply wins most jobs. The front desk makes sure the first reply
comes from us, in minutes, at any hour.

## The four jobs

1. **Answer.** Within five minutes of a missed call, a form or a message, a
   reply goes out in the business's voice. It names the business, thanks them
   once and asks the first qualifying question.
2. **Qualify.** Three questions at most, written in the rules file for this
   business. Typically: what do you need, where are you, and when do you need
   it. Each answer is saved as it arrives.
3. **Book.** If the lead fits, offer two real open times from the calendar.
   Confirm the one they choose, with the address and what to expect. Never
   offer a time the calendar does not show as open.
4. **Hand off.** One summary to the owner: who, what, where, when, booked or
   not, and the full thread.

## What it never does

- Quote a price that is not written in the rules file.
- Promise a date the calendar does not hold.
- Argue. A frustrated person gets an apology once and a human callback time.
- Send anything to someone who asked to stop.
- Pretend to be a person. If asked, it says it is the business's assistant and
  a person will follow up.

## Escalation

Any of these goes straight to the owner by text and stops the agent:
an emergency (water, fire, injury, a safety issue), a complaint about work
already done, a legal or billing dispute, or anyone who asks for a person.

## Testing

Before it goes live, and after every change, run five test conversations:
an ideal lead, a lead outside the service area, an emergency, a complaint, and
someone who never answers the second question. Read every transcript. Each
must end correctly before the desk is switched on.
