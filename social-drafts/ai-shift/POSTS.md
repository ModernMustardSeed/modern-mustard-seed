# The AI Shift: group posts

Six graphics, 1080x1350 in `cards/` and 1080x1080 in `cards-square/` for X.
Direction is "The Signal", approved from `moodboard.png` on 2026-07-29. It is a
deliberate break from the screenprint of the first two sets: near-black
broadcast card, high-voltage lime, an oscilloscope trace generated per card.

**Why dark:** a Facebook or LinkedIn feed is a wall of white. These stop the
thumb because they are the only black thing on the screen.

**The CTA is a dare that costs nothing:** open ChatGPT, ask for the best in your
trade in your town, see whose name comes back. Everybody can do it in ten
seconds, and the ones who are missing feel it immediately. The audit link is the
follow-through, and it goes in the FIRST COMMENT, never the post body.

## Three hard rules on this set

1. **Never say "45% start with AI."** BrightLocal found 45% *use* AI somewhere in
   the journey. DreamHost found only **3%** *start* there. Card 03 exists
   specifically to say this out loud before somebody else does.
2. **No ranking promises.** Not for Google, not for ChatGPT. Nobody can promise
   placement in a model's answer, and anyone who does is guessing.
3. **Disclose DreamHost.** They sell hosting, so they benefit from "you need a
   website." Say it yourself when you cite them.

## Post 2: the stat teardown
**Graphic:** `01-the-shift.png`, or run 01 + 02 as a two-image carousel

> Last year, 6% of people used AI to help find a local business. This year it is
> 45%. That is BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US consumers.
>
> Before anybody panics: most of them are not starting there. Only about 3% open
> AI first (DreamHost, 2026). They still start on Google. But somewhere in the
> middle of deciding, nearly half of them are now asking a machine what it
> thinks.
>
> Here is the part that actually matters. That machine is reading websites. Not
> your Facebook page, not your reputation around town, not how good your work is.
> It reads what is written down and it recommends what it can understand.
>
> If your site does not say plainly what you do and where you do it, you are not
> being rejected. You are just not in the room.

## Post 4: what to actually do about it
**Graphic:** `06-readable.png`

> Every week somebody asks me what they have to do to "show up in AI." Most of
> the answers being sold are nonsense. Here is the honest version, and it is
> boring on purpose.
>
> 1. Say plainly on your website what you do and which towns you drive to. In
>    words. Not in a logo, not in a slideshow, not in a photo of a truck.
> 2. Make your hours, phone and address match your Google profile exactly.
>    Mismatches make you look closed or fake to anything reading you.
> 3. Write down the questions customers actually ask you. Do you do emergencies.
>    Are you licensed. How fast can you get here. Put the answers in text.
>
> That is most of it. Nobody can promise you a spot in an AI answer, and be
> careful with anybody who does. What you can do is be readable, and a startling
> number of local businesses currently are not.
