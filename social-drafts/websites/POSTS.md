# The Storefront Files: group posts

Six graphics, 1080x1350 (4:5). Works as-is on Facebook feed, Facebook groups,
Instagram feed, and LinkedIn. Files in `cards/`.

**The CTA on every card:** the free website audit at
modernmustardseed.com/website-audit. Verified 2026-07-28 against
`app/website-audit/page.tsx`: free, no credit card, no email needed to see the
result, 30 to 60 seconds, score 0-100 plus a ranked to-do list.

The URL is printed **on the card art**, not in the post body. An image is not a
link, so this keeps full Facebook reach the same way the phone number does in
[the Missed Call Files](../missed-calls/POSTS.md). If you want the clickable
link, put it in the FIRST COMMENT, never the body.

⚠️ **Never quote a website price.** The Seed Site and Full-Service Business Build
are both "scoped and quoted after a free discovery call." There is no list price
to type, and typing one from memory breaks the single-source price rule.

⚠️ **No ranking promises.** Not "we get you to page one." The audit scores what
is on the page. That is the claim.

## Read this before you post in someone else's group

Same rule as the phone set: **the dare, not the demo**. You are not selling a
website. You are handing them a free grader and inviting them to disagree with
it. Lead with Post 1. The stat posts are the follow-up, not the opener.

Serve for a week in a group before you post one of these.

## Post 2: the stat teardown (value first, the softest ask)
**Graphic:** `01-google-first.png`, or run 01 + 02 + 03 as a 3-image carousel

> 67% of people start at Google when they need someone local. Not a referral. Not
> the phone book. Google.
>
> Then 58% go look at your website to check whether you are actually real.
>
> Both numbers are from DreamHost's 2026 Trust Index, 1,201 US consumers. Worth
> knowing they sell hosting, so read them with that in mind. But the shape
> matches what every owner I talk to already sees happening.
>
> Here is the part that gets missed. Your Facebook page is the window display.
> People look through it, and then they go somewhere else to decide. If there is
> nowhere to go, 39% of them just leave. Another 45% say a business with no
> website does not feel real to them.
>
> Three fixes, in the order I would do them:
>
> 1. Claim and fill out your Google Business Profile. It is free and it is the
>    single highest-leverage hour you will spend.
> 2. Get one honest page up with what you do, where you work, and how to reach
>    you. One page beats no page by a mile.
> 3. Put the phone number in the top right where a thumb can hit it.
>
> None of that requires hiring anybody.

## Post 3: the objection killer (for the "word of mouth is enough" crowd)
**Graphic:** `03-no-site.png`

> "I get all my work from word of mouth, I do not need a website."
>
> I believe you. Word of mouth is the best lead source there is and I would never
> tell you otherwise.
>
> But watch what actually happens. Your happy customer tells their neighbor about
> you. The neighbor does not call. The neighbor searches your name first. That is
> the moment. If nothing comes up, the referral you earned quietly dies on the
> sidewalk.
>
> 39% of consumers say they have walked away from a business that had no website
> (DreamHost, 2026). Not argued. Walked.
>
> The website is not there to get you new customers. It is there to stop you from
> losing the ones you already earned.

## Post 4: the part almost nobody has adjusted to yet
**Graphic:** `04-ai-front-door.png`

> Last year, 6% of people used AI to find a local business. This year it is 45%.
> That is BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 1,002 US consumers.
>
> One caveat so nobody quotes this at me wrong: most of them are not starting
> there. They still start at Google and use AI somewhere in the middle. But 45%
> of people are now, at some point, asking a machine which local business to use.
>
> Here is what that changes. Something is reading every website in your town and
> deciding which three names to say out loud. It cannot recommend a business it
> cannot read. No site, no mention. Thin site, no mention.
>
> The businesses that sort this out in the next year are going to look very lucky
> in three years.

comments, then the next. Post 1 is the opener everywhere. Post 3 is the one that
gets shared, because every owner has had the word-of-mouth argument.

week. They are the same person selling two things, and it reads that way.
